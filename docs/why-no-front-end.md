# Why there is no front end

Naranga was a database with a subscription, and in November 2025 its rivals reported the software being wound down. The records underneath it were ordinary: franchisees, sites, agreements, monthly sales, field visits, tickets and leads. What franchisors paid for was the layer on top that let people who do not write SQL get at those records. Screens, filters, dashboards, forms. When that layer goes away, the franchisor is left asking for an export.

That layer used to be the whole product, because talking to a database was hard. It is not hard any more. Open this folder in Claude Code, describe what you want, and it writes the query, runs it, and explains the answer. Ask a question the dashboard never had a chart for and you still get an answer.

## What you gain

- **Better answers.** A dashboard shows what the vendor decided to chart. Here you ask "which sites are down on the quarter and also behind on royalties?" and get it answered against your own records.
- **No per-location fee.** Add a site and the bill does not move.
- **Your records in your Postgres.** Plain tables. Back them up, query them from anything. If a vendor disappears, nothing disappears with it.
- **The Code's clocks built in.** The 14 day disclosure window, cooling off, the end of term notice, the fund statement. Rules you change in one file when the Code changes.

## What you give up

- **A field app.** Field managers on the road want a phone screen with a checklist and a camera. Here a visit is recorded after the fact, by typing or by asking Claude Code. Enterprise DNA builds that phone screen on the same database when a network needs it.
- **A franchisee portal.** Franchisees cannot log in to report sales or raise tickets. Today head office records them from email or a spreadsheet. A simple portal is a customisation.
- **An online training library.** Course hosting and video are not here. Certificates and inductions are recorded; the courses live wherever they live now.
- **A vendor help desk.** This is open source. Enterprise DNA supports the installed version for networks that want someone to call.

## Who this fits

Franchisors with five to fifty sites where head office runs the network from a desk, and where the questions matter more than the screens. If your field team needs a phone app on day one, start with the free version at head office and have the phone screen built next.
